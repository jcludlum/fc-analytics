# Leek Tart

In the village where I grew up in Rheinland-Pfalz in southwestern Germany, the fare is simple and rustic. But it's an adaptable and variable cuisine. That's because our curious cooks love to peek into the cooking pots of our French neighbors, pilfer their recipes, adjust them to our own taste, and then call them echt pfaelzisch, or genuine pfaelzisch.

One of these adaptations is the leek tart, a flat yeast bread topped with a moist, creamy leek-and-bacon custard. I think of it as a hybrid of Alsatian Flammkuchen, quiche Lorraine, and German Zwiebelkuchen, or onion tart. Leek tarts remind me of my childhood: we'd eat them after the grape harvest with a tangy, fizzy, new, white Federweisser wine.

To make the soft, bread-like crust that's characteristic of German tarts, you simply add eggs, butter, and milk to enrich the yeast dough. The custard filling isn't difficult to make, but inattention at two points can ruin the topping. First, the leeks must be thoroughly cleaned so that no grit gets into the custard. Second, the cooked leeks, bacon, and cream must be cool when the eggs are added. Otherwise, the eggs cook immediately and make the custard lumpy.

Now that I live in California, I find many occasions to bake a leek tart. It's good warm or at room temperature. It makes a wonderful lunch dish, served with a soup or green salad. My American friends enjoy it as a party food with a cool glass of Pilsner or a dry white wine, and my children like to take it to school for lunch.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup milk
- 2 eggs
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1/2 teaspoon sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 package dry yeast or cake of compressed yeast
- 4 cups flour
- 6 leeks
- 1/2 pound bacon
- 1 cup cream
- 1 teaspoon caraway seeds
- Salt
- pepper
- 3 eggs
- 1 egg yolk

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Warm the milk, eggs, and butter before making the dough, since a warm dough rises faster: submerge the eggs in hot water for about 10 minutes, heat the milk until lukewarm, and soften the butter at room temperature.
2. Pour the milk into a large bowl and dissolve the sugar and salt in it. Sprinkle or crumble the yeast on top of the milk and let stand 2 to 3 minutes to rehydrate.
3. Add the eggs, the butter (cut into small pieces), and 1 cup of flour, and beat with a wooden spoon or an electric mixer.
4. Add the second cup of flour and continue to stir. When the flour is absorbed, pour in the third cup; at this point the dough will be stiff and heavy.
5. Mix the dough with your hands, or switch to the dough hook if using an electric mixer. Gradually sprinkle on more flour and work it in until the dough is no longer sticky and pulls away from the sides of the bowl.
6. Knead the dough until it's smooth and satiny, about 15 to 20 minutes by hand, or about 3 minutes with a dough hook.
7. Turn the dough over in a greased bowl, cover, and let it rise in a warm place until it doubles in size, about an hour. Punch down the dough and knead it again briefly to release trapped air.
8. Roll the dough on a lightly floured surface until it's slightly larger than your baking sheet and about 1/2-inch thick (let the dough rest about 10 minutes first to let the gluten relax).
9. Use your fingers to form a 1-inch-high lip around the rectangle so the custard topping can't run out.
10. Cover the baking sheet with a damp cloth and let the crust rise again in a warm place for 20 minutes.
11. Clean and slice the leeks: cut off the roots, the bottom 1/4 inch of the stalk, and 2/3 of the dark-green tops. Starting from the root end, cut lengthwise halfway through the stem, then pull apart and fan out the leaves and wash under fast-running water.
12. Slice the leeks into thin rounds, about 1/8-inch thick, using all of the white part and a bit of the dark-green leaves for color. If the leeks are particularly gritty, wash them again after slicing, soaking in a salad spinner, and spin dry.
13. Cut the bacon into small pieces, about 1/4 inch square, and fry in a large pot until crisp. Drain the bacon on a paper towel and remove all but about 1 tablespoon of fat from the pot.
14. Saute the leeks in the bacon fat over low heat for 3 to 5 minutes until soft, then put the bacon back in the pot with the leeks.
15. Stir in the cream and caraway seeds and season with salt and pepper to taste. Simmer over low heat for about 5 minutes to blend the flavors.
16. Let the mixture cool, then stir in the eggs. (Adding them while the mixture is still hot will cook them and make tough lumps.)
17. Brush the rim of the risen crust with a beaten egg yolk. Spread the topping on the crust and bake in a 350°F oven until the custard is set and the crust is golden brown, 25 to 35 minutes.
