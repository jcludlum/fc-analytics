# Orange Sponge Cakes in a Roasted Pistachio Crust

These cakes are a variation of the lemon-and-almond sponge cake and have an appealing bright-green color. Pistachio nuts imported from Turkey and Afghanistan are smaller than those grown in California and have a brighter color. Serves eight.

## Ingredients

- 1 oz. (2 Tbs.) unsalted butter, softened
- 7 oz. (1 1/2 cups) unsalted pistachio nuts, shelled and skinned
- 2 1/4 oz. (1/2 cup plus 1 Tbs.) cake flour
- 2 oz. (1/4 cup plus 2 Tbs.) cornstarch
- 2 eggs
- 5 egg yolks
- 5 oz. (1/2 cup plus 2 Tbs.) sugar
- 1/2 tsp. vanilla
- 1 1/2 oz. (3 Tbs.) unsalted butter, melted and cooled
- 1 Tbs. orange zest
- 2 oz. (1/4 cup) water
- 2 oz. (1/4 cup) sugar
- 1 tsp. orange liqueur, such as Grand Marnier
- 1/4 cup confectioners' sugar
- 1 oz. white chocolate

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Position the rack in the lower third of the oven and heat to 325°F. Butter the bottom and sides of eight 3- by 2-in. tartlet pans well with the softened butter.
2. If using salted pistachios, blanch them in boiling water for 30 seconds to remove the salt, then dry them in a 300°F oven for 5 min. and let cool.
3. Arrange pistachio nuts in the tartlet pans, flat side down, to form a decorative pattern.
4. Put the remaining pistachios in a food processor fitted with the blade attachment and pulse until finely chopped.
5. Put the chopped pistachios in a medium-fine sieve and sift them over a piece of waxed paper, letting the ground pistachios fall through the sieve. Reserve the ground pistachios.
6. Put the chopped pistachios in the prepared pans and gently roll each pan, coating the bottom and sides with the nuts. If the pans have removable bottoms, hold the bottoms in place with a finger while turning the pans upside down to remove any excess.
7. Grate the orange zest.
8. In a medium bowl, sift the flour, cornstarch, and reserved ground pistachios together.
9. In the bowl of a heavy-duty electric mixer fitted with the wire whip attachment, beat the eggs and yolks until blended.
10. Gradually add the sugar and continue beating until the mixture is pale yellow and leaves a thick ribbon trail when the whip is lifted, 10 to 15 min. Beat in the vanilla and orange zest.
11. Using a large rubber spatula, gently fold a third of the flour mixture into the beaten eggs. Gently fold in the remaining flour mixture, a third at a time.
12. Put about 2 cups of the batter in a medium bowl and gently fold in the melted, cooled butter. Return the mixture to the rest of the batter and fold just until combined.
13. Scrape the batter into the prepared pans and bake for 15 min. Cool the cakes completely in the pans on a wire rack.
14. To make the sugar syrup, combine the water and sugar in a small saucepan. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly with a wooden spoon until the sugar dissolves. Raise the heat to medium-high and bring to a boil. Cool the syrup to room temperature, then stir in the orange liqueur.
15. Unmold the cakes and brush each with the orange-liqueur syrup. Let them sit for 1 hour to absorb the syrup.
16. Center an upside-down round tartlet pan, about 1/2 in. less in diameter than the top of each cake, on top of each cake. Put the confectioners' sugar in a fine sieve and dust each cake lightly with the sugar.
17. Melt the white chocolate over hot (not simmering) water and cool to room temperature. Dip the bottom of each whole pistachio into the melted chocolate and place it in the middle of a cake. The white chocolate holds the pistachio on the cake and should be barely visible.
