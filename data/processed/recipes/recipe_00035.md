# Chicken Broth

Chicken broth is often confused with chicken stock. While the two use similar ingredients and are often interchangeable, there are important differences. Broth uses chicken meat in its preparation, while stock uses chicken bones, or a combination of bones and parts. Broth is a clear, full-flavored liquid generally served as a soup, with a few additions or garnishes, so a more pronounced flavor is desired.

## Ingredients

- 1 whole chicken (about 3 lb.), including the gizzard, neck, and heart, or an equal weight of chicken backs, necks, and trimmings
- 2 onions, peeled and quartered
- 4 carrots, scrubbed and cut in 2-inch pieces
- 4 ribs celery, scrubbed and cut in 2-inch pieces
- 1 Tbs. whole black peppercorns
- Several parsley sprigs
- 1 to 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- Cold water, to cover
- Salt, a little, to taste

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Put a whole chicken (about 3 lb.), including the gizzard, neck, and heart but not the liver, into a large pot. (Instead of a whole chicken, you can use an equal weight of chicken backs, necks, and trimmings, including gizzards and hearts.)
2. Add the onions, carrots, celery, peppercorns, and a bouquet garni made by tying together the parsley sprigs, thyme, and bay leaf with string.
3. Add cold water to cover. Add only a little salt at this point, because as the liquid cooks and reduces, the salt becomes more pronounced.
4. Bring the liquid to a boil, then immediately reduce the heat to low so that the liquid gently simmers.
5. As scum rises to the surface, skim it off with a spoon or skimmer and discard it. Skim occasionally as the broth cooks, since skimming is vital to the clarity of the finished broth.
6. Simmer for two to three hours, adding enough water as needed to keep the ingredients covered.
7. If using a whole chicken, remove it as soon as it's cooked, after about one hour. Cool it slightly, cut off the meat, and return the carcass to the pot.
8. Strain the finished broth through a fine sieve, ideally a conical sieve. If you don't have a very fine-mesh sieve, first strain the broth through any strainer, then pass it through a sieve lined with a double thickness of paper towel, changing the paper towels several times as they become coated with impurities.
9. Defat the broth before serving. If you have time, refrigerate it until completely cold so the fat rises and hardens, then remove it with a spoon. If you don't have time to chill the broth, remove as much fat as possible from the surface with a ladle, then drag a paper towel over the surface to absorb the remaining fat.
