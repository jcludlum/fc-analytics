# Lamb Loin & Spring Vegetables

When buying the lamb, be sure to specify lamb loin on the bone. Whether your butcher bones the lamb or you do, save the bones and meat scraps to make the lamb stock. The sauce for the lamb is light and brothy, just the ticket for springtime. To drink with the lamb, I would pour a red wine, perhaps a young merlot with lots of fruit and mild tannins. Serves six.

## Ingredients

- 3 lamb racks from the loin
- 2 Tbs. olive oil
- Salt
- freshly ground pepper
- About a cupful of each of at least five spring vegetables, selected for a variety of colors: small new potatoes, fava beans, small carrots and turnips, sugar snap peas, English peas, tiny crookneck squash, young golden beets, little creamer onions, baby leeks, French string beans (haricots verts), whole immature garlic bulbs, small zucchini
- Extra-virgin olive oil
- Salt
- freshly ground white pepper
- 2 cups lamb stock ***
- 3 shallots, peeled and chopped fine
- 2 to 3 Tbs. unsalted butter
- 1/2 cup chopped fresh herbs-half tarragon, the rest parsley and chives

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. If your butcher hasn't already boned the lamb racks, bone them yourself: place each loin fat side down on the cutting board and, keeping the knife blade tilted slightly toward the bone, cut away the smaller filet. The bone is T-shaped; complete freeing the filet by cutting down the leg of the T. Turn the piece around and free the loin by working around the bottom of the T, feeling with your fingers for where the bones start, then cutting back up the other side of the leg. Carefully cut around the half-inch bump where the bone turns at the top of the T, then cut the loin completely from the bone. Separate the loin from its shell of fat with small cuts of the knife tip, rolling the loin toward you as you free it, until only a thin layer of tough "silver skin" remains. Remove the silver skin from both the filet and the loin, and trim away any remaining bits of fat. Each rack yields two tubular pieces of meat: a larger, flatter one from the loin and a smaller one from the filet.
2. Trim the meat, cutting away all fat and silver skin, wrap in plastic, and refrigerate. Discard the fat but save all the meat trimmings, scraps, and bones; break the bones into small pieces with a heavy cleaver (or have the butcher saw them up) and use them to make the lamb stock.
3. Prepare the vegetables as required and arrange them, covered, in separate bowls so you can add each to the steamer at the right moment. Set up the vegetable steamer.
4. Two hours before serving, take the lamb out of the refrigerator, salt and pepper it generously, and set aside, covered, so it rises to room temperature.
5. Fifteen minutes before serving the asparagus, heat the olive oil in a heavy skillet large enough to hold the meat without crowding (use two pans if necessary). Over medium to high heat, saute the large loin pieces, pushing and turning them frequently so they brown and cook evenly. After 3 or 4 min., add the filet pieces and continue cooking; the room-temperature lamb cooks quickly, particularly the filets. Cook until pink but not blood rare, undercooking slightly since the meat will continue to cook as it rests.
6. Place the cooked lamb on a rack set over a plate, cover loosely with aluminum foil, and keep warm.
7. Drain the grease from the skillet and, back on the heat, add the shallots, then the lamb stock, bringing it to a boil while scraping up the caramelized cooking juices from the bottom of the pan. Cook for a few minutes, taste, and reduce further if needed.
8. Just before serving, steam the vegetables, starting with the longest-cooking ones (such as potatoes, young garlic heads, and creamer onions) and adding the quicker-cooking ones (such as zucchini and fresh fava beans) last, so everything finishes at the same time.
9. Toss the cooked vegetables with a couple of teaspoons of olive oil (or butter), just enough to make them glisten, and season liberally with salt and pepper.
10. Bring the sauce back to a simmer and season with salt and pepper. Add the juice that has drained from the resting meat and, just before serving, swirl in the butter and half the chopped herbs.
11. Slice each large loin crosswise into six pieces and each filet into as many pieces as there are guests. Divide the sauce among warm dinner plates, arrange the loin slices and a piece of filet in the center of each, scatter the vegetables around the meat, sprinkle with the remaining herbs, and serve.
