# San Francisco Sourdough

Five years ago, after living in Northern California for fifteen years, I moved back to the East Coast. Soon I began to long for the soul-satisfying tang of San Francisco sourdough bread. Unable to locate a good source for truly sour and crusty loaves, I decided to make it myself. Two hundred batches later, I'm happy to report that it's possible to make world-class sourdough breads at home.

## Ingredients

- 6 cups unbleached bread flour
- 2 cups San Francisco starter
- 1 cup potato starter
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 3 tablespoons nonfat dry milk
- 1 tablespoon salt
- Cornmeal, for the peel
- About a cup of water, for steam

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. If starting a culture from scratch, mix two cups of flour with two cups of either warm water, yogurt, milk, or the water potatoes have been cooked in, in a glass or ceramic bowl. Cover with a clean dishtowel or cheesecloth and let sit in a warm spot for three to four days, stirring once a day, until it bubbles and has a pleasant, sour smell. Then feed the culture a mixture of flour and water (or thinned mashed potatoes, for a potato starter) until you have enough active starter to work with. Discard and start over if it turns moldy or develops a peculiar smell.
2. Maintain two starters: a San Francisco starter, fed weekly with equal parts unbleached white flour and water to the consistency of warm yogurt, and a potato starter, fed every two weeks with boiled potatoes, water, and sugar blended to the consistency of a milk shake. Let the San Francisco starter sit at room temperature for at least 48 hours after feeding to develop sufficient tang before using it or refrigerating the rest.
3. Combine the flour, San Francisco starter, potato starter, olive oil, nonfat dry milk, and salt, and knead until the dough is satiny and smooth, about 15 minutes.
4. Let the dough rise until about 2 1/2 times its original size, 8 to 12 hours depending on temperature and how active the starters are.
5. Punch down the dough to release trapped air, divide it in half, and form each half into a round loaf. Put the loaves on a peel well-covered with cornmeal, cover with a moist towel, and let rise until doubled, 4 to 6 hours.
6. Preheat a pizza stone on the bottom of the oven for 45 minutes at 500°F. Five minutes before baking, move the stone to the bottom shelf, put a pizza pan on the oven floor, and turn the heat down to 425°F.
7. Slash the top of each loaf with a razor blade and slide the loaves onto the baking stone. Immediately pour the water into the pizza pan and quickly shut the oven door to trap the steam.
8. Bake until the crust is very dark, 50 minutes to an hour.
9. Once a loaf has cooled completely, it can be sealed in a plastic bag and frozen; thaw at room temperature for about 2 hours before serving.
