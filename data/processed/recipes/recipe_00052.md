# Roasted Chicken with Honey-Thyme Vinaigrette

Serves four.

## Ingredients

- 6 small shallots, sliced
- 1/2 cup red-wine vinegar
- 1 large bunch fresh thyme
- 1/2 cup olive oil
- 3-pound chicken
- 3 tablespoons coarse salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 4 medium ribs celery, chopped
- 1 medium onion, chopped
- 1 tablespoon lavender honey or other floral honey
- Mixed salad greens (such as romaine, Bibb, frisee, red oak leaf), washed and torn in pieces (optional)

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. At least a day before (up to a week), combine the sliced shallots and vinegar in a bowl, cover, and refrigerate. Once it has enough shallot flavor, strain and refrigerate the vinegar until ready to use. (If short on time, add the shallots directly to the roasting pan when deglazing with the vinegar instead.)
2. Strip the thyme leaves from the stems (aiming for about a third of a cup loosely packed leaves). Combine the thyme and olive oil in a blender and process until the thyme is completely incorporated into the oil. Cover and refrigerate the thyme oil, straining it before using. Use within three days.
3. Remove any giblets from the chicken and pull off all excess fat. Rub the skin and cavity with the coarse salt, let sit for about an hour, then wipe off the excess salt (this step is optional but improves flavor and texture).
4. Heat the oven to 500°F. Brush the chicken generously with some of the thyme oil and season with freshly ground black pepper.
5. Put the celery and onion in a flameproof roasting pan, season with salt and pepper, and set the chicken on top of the vegetables.
6. Roast at 500°F for 15 minutes, then reduce the heat to 350°F and continue cooking until the juices from the cavity run clear when the chicken is lifted, another 45 to 50 minutes. If the breast meat is browning too quickly, cover it with one or two hardy lettuce leaves, such as romaine.
7. Remove the chicken from the roasting pan and keep it in a warm place.
8. Put the roasting pan directly on a burner over medium-high heat. Add the vinegar to the vegetables and chicken juices in the pan (add the shallots too, if not already infused into the vinegar) and boil until the liquid is reduced by about half, scraping to dissolve any drippings from the bottom of the pan.
9. Strain the liquid into a small, deep-sided bowl, pressing on the vegetables with a spoon to extract their juices. Whisk in the honey and taste; if the sauce is too sharp, whisk in a little more thyme oil to mellow it. Season to taste with salt and pepper.
10. Serve the chicken on a bed of the mixed salad greens tossed with some of the honey-thyme vinaigrette, if desired.
