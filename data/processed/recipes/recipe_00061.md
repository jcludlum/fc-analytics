# Tomato-Sage Intermezzo Granita

I like this best during a seafood dinner. Use the most flavorful tomatoes you can find. Stirring slows down the freezing process, so make this granita two days before you plan to serve it. You really have to watch the sweetness when making an intermezzo granita. If the mixture tastes too sweet, correct with more lemon juice and salt. Makes about 4 cups.

## Ingredients

- 3 lb. plum tomatoes
- 3 lb. medium-size standard tomatoes
- 3/4 tsp. coarse salt
- 1/4 tsp. black pepper
- 2 tsp. extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 to 1 1/2 cups sage-infused simple syrup ***
- Juice of 1 1/2 lemons
- Juice of 2 limes
- 2 cups water
- 1 cup sugar
- 8 to 10 clean, fresh sage leaves

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Score the stem end of the tomatoes and drop them, a few at a time, into a large pot of boiling water for 90 seconds. Transfer to a large bowl of ice water to cool.
2. Peel, cut in half, remove all the seeds, and cut away any core. Puree the tomatoes in a blender with the coarse salt, black pepper, and olive oil. Strain the puree, reserving both the pulp (about 3 cups) and the juice.
3. In a heavy, nonreactive pot, combine the reserved tomato juice, the water, and the sugar. Tie the sage leaves in cheesecloth or a muslin infusion bag and add to the pot.
4. Set over medium-high heat and bring to a boil, stirring frequently. Remove from the heat and let steep 20 min., then remove the sage and let the syrup cool.
5. Whisk 1 cup of the sage syrup into the tomato pulp. Add the lemon or lime juice to taste, and more salt if necessary, whisking together. If the mixture coats a wooden spoon thinly, it's ready; if the coating is heavy, whisk in more syrup.
6. Transfer to a plastic container and freeze, stirring every 30 min. until the mixture starts to become solid, then leave to freeze completely (about 24 hours total).
7. Scrape with a large spoon or ice-cream scoop into a chilled bowl to serve.
