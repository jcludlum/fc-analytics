# Demi-Sel (Salt-Cured Pork)

Literally, demi-sel means half-salt. In other words, salt makes up about half the curing ingredients that preserve the pork. Sugar and saltpeter are included as well. Curing the pork in this sweet brine for a week gives the meat a smooth texture, pink color, and fresh sweet-salt flavor. Regardless of how you use demi-sel, it's important to keep in mind that this curing method is not a substitute for cooking-the pork is still fundamentally raw and has to be fully cooked before eating.

## Ingredients

- 12 oz. sea salt
- 12 oz. sugar
- 4 heaping tablespoons saltpeter
- 3 quarts unchlorinated bottled water
- 5 to 6 lb. pork (belly, shoulder, leg, or loin)

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Combine the sea salt, sugar, and saltpeter in the water in a large pot. Bring to a boil, stirring occasionally, then skim off and discard any froth. Let cool.
2. Pour the brine into a large crockery, glass, or plastic container through a cloth-lined sieve to strain out any impurities or undissolved salts.
3. Add the pork and submerge it by weighing it down with a board. Refrigerate.
4. After six days, remove the demi-sel. It's ready to cook.
