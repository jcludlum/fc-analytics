# Ganache

Ganache is wonderful. Rich chocolate is mixed into hot cream, and as it melts, fine droplets of cocoa butter disperse into the cream, forming a shiny, silky-smooth mixture. Ganache's uses are many because, by varying the proportion of cream to chocolate, it can be made thin and pourable or thick and dense, or even whipped into an airy, mousselike consistency. For the intense flavor and dense texture needed for truffles, use anywhere from 7 to 8 1/2 ounces of cream for every 12 ounces of chocolate. For a rich cake or pastry glaze that's solid enough to form a thin, hard shell yet soft enough to cut through, equal parts chocolate and cream work well. Ganaches for cake fillings and frostings are usually light and airy, yet firm enough to support the layers above-heavier butter cakes call for equal parts chocolate and cream, while lighter sponge cakes such as a genoise can use up to twice as much cream as chocolate.

## Ingredients

- Bittersweet, semisweet, sweet, milk, or white bar chocolate, finely chopped
- Heavy cream
- Half-and-half (optional, blended with the heavy cream to lighten richness)

## Directions

1. Chop the chocolate: break it into small pieces with the tip of a knife, then chop it into smaller granules in a food processor, pulsing on and off to avoid overheating and melting it. (If you don't have a food processor, slice the chocolate into thick shavings with a knife; don't use a potato peeler or a grater, since the very thin pieces they produce melt too quickly.)
2. Slowly bring the cream to a full boil and let it simmer for about 15 seconds. Transfer the cream to a large round-bottomed metal bowl and let it cool for about a minute.
3. Pour the chopped chocolate onto the hot cream and wait for it to start melting around the edges before mixing it in.
4. Using a plastic or rubber spatula, stir a small amount of chocolate in the center of the bowl into the hot cream with an energetic, circular motion until it becomes shiny. Gradually widen the circles, stirring relentlessly until all the chocolate is melted and the mixture is smooth, glossy, and homogeneous.
5. If any chocolate remains unmelted as the cream cools, set the bowl of ganache inside another bowl filled with warm tap water (90° to 95°F) and stir slowly and gently until it melts in.
6. For a silky-smooth ganache, pour the mixture through a fine sieve to remove any coagulated bits of cream and air bubbles.
7. If not using right away, press a piece of plastic wrap directly onto the surface to prevent a crust from forming. Let cool completely before refrigerating. (Properly emulsified ganache keeps about a day at room temperature, a week in the refrigerator, and up to a month in the freezer.)
8. To flavor, steep fresh herbs, seeds, citrus peels, vanilla beans, coffee, or tea in the cream before mixing in the chocolate, or mix a liqueur, softened butter, sweetened fruit puree, soft caramel, dried or candied fruit, or nuts into the ganache once it has cooled completely but hasn't set (after an hour or two at room temperature).
9. To whip ganache into a mousselike consistency, beat it briefly with a whisk once it's cold and firm.
