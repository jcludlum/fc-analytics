# Triple Chocolate Cake

This cake combines three chocolate flavors-cocoa in the cake layers, semisweet chocolate in the whipped filling, and a less sweet bittersweet chocolate in the glaze. While the contrast gives the cake complexity, you can use the same chocolate for the filling and the glaze if you can't find both semisweet and bittersweet chocolate. All the elements can be made one to two days in advance. In fact, the assembled cake will keep well for a day in the refrigerator.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups (16 oz.) heavy cream
- 8 oz. semisweet chocolate, chopped fine
- 4 large eggs
- 3/4 cup (5 oz.) sugar
- 1 cup (4 oz.) all purpose flour
- 1/4 cup (1 oz.) cocoa powder
- 1/4 cup (2 oz.) butter, clarified
- 1/2 cup water
- 1/2 cup (3 1/2 oz.) sugar
- 1 to 2 Tbs. rum or other liqueur
- 1 cup (8 oz.) heavy cream
- 2 Tbs. light corn syrup (optional)
- 8 oz. bittersweet chocolate, chopped fine

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Make the ganache filling: in a small, heavy saucepan, bring the heavy cream to a boil, then transfer it to a large bowl. Let the cream cool briefly (about 1 min.), then pour the chopped semisweet chocolate on top and let it begin to melt. Slowly stir the chocolate and cream in a circular motion, working out from the center of the bowl, until all the chocolate is melted and the ganache is smooth and glossy. Cover the surface with plastic wrap and refrigerate until well chilled, at least two hours.
2. Make the chocolate genoise cake: heat the oven to 350°F. Grease and flour a 9- by 2-in. round cake pan.
3. In a large mixing bowl set over a pan of simmering water, whisk together the eggs and sugar until the sugar has dissolved and the mixture feels warm to the touch. Remove from the heat and, using a hand mixer or the wire whip on a countertop mixer, whip the eggs at high speed until thick and a ribbon trail forms when the beater is lifted.
4. Sift together the flour and cocoa powder. With a rubber spatula, gently fold the flour mixture into the whipped eggs and sugar, mixing only until incorporated. Fold in the lukewarm clarified butter.
5. Immediately pour the batter into the prepared pan and bake on the middle rack for 30 to 35 min., until the top springs back when lightly pressed. Turn the cake out onto a rack and let cool completely.
6. Make the syrup: in a small, heavy saucepan, heat the water and sugar over medium heat, stirring until the sugar is dissolved. Increase the heat and boil until the syrup is completely clear, about 1 min. Remove from the heat and, once cool, stir in the rum or other liqueur.
7. Make the ganache glaze following the same method as the filling: bring the heavy cream (with the corn syrup, if using) to a boil, then pour it over the chopped bittersweet chocolate and stir gently until smooth (stirring gently helps prevent air bubbles, which are very noticeable on the glazed cake). Firmly tap the bowl on the counter several times to release any bubbles that do form, and cover with plastic wrap.
8. To assemble, place the cake on a 9-inch cardboard cake circle and slice it with a serrated knife into two even layers. Using a pastry brush, lightly moisten the bottom layer with half the syrup.
9. With a wire whisk or the whip attachment on an electric mixer, whip the chilled ganache filling just until stiff peaks form.
10. Spread a third of the whipped ganache on the bottom cake layer and cap it with the top layer. Brush the top with the remaining syrup, then spread the remaining filling over the top and sides of the cake, making the surface as smooth as possible. Chill the cake in the refrigerator for at least an hour before glazing.
11. Check the glaze's consistency by pouring a little over your fingernail; if you can see the outline of your nail through it, the consistency is right (if not, thin it by heating it over a saucepan of gently simmering water).
12. Place the cake on a wire rack set on a rimmed baking sheet. Pour the glaze onto the center of the cake and let it run down the sides, quickly scraping up glaze from the baking sheet to cover any gaps that remain. Once the cake is completely covered, pick it up (with the rack and baking sheet) and gently tap it on the counter to dislodge any air bubbles, immediately popping any remaining bubbles with a pin. Don't try to smooth the glaze at this point.
13. Chill the cake in the refrigerator until ready to serve.
