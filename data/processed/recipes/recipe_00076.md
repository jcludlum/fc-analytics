# Bangkok-Style Glass Noodles

The dried glass noodles called for in this recipe are thin, brittle, milky-white noodles made from mung bean starch. They're usually sold in small bundles. The lily buds, cloud ear mushrooms, and pickled garlic add an authentic note to this popular Bangkok dish, but by all means make this stir-fry even if you can't find these ingredients. Serves six.

## Ingredients

- 6 oz. dried glass noodles (also called cellophane noodles, mung bean thread noodles, or Chinese vermicelli)
- 1/2 cup dried lily buds (also called golden needles)
- 1/2 Tbs. dried cloud ear mushrooms
- 1/2 cup vegetable oil
- 1 Tbs. minced garlic
- 1 cup thinly sliced raw chicken breast (about 1 lb.)
- Salt
- 3 eggs
- 1/2 cup julienned celery, cut 2-in. long
- 1 cup finely shredded green cabbage
- 1/2 cup finely julienned carrots, cut 2-in. long
- 1 Tbs. dark soy sauce (see-ewe dum)
- sweet soy sauce (see-ewe wan)
- 3 heads sweet pickled garlic, sliced thick horizontally
- 3 heads roasted garlic, sliced thick horizontally
- 1/4 cup soy sauce
- 1 tsp. white pepper
- 2 tsp. sugar
- 2 cups chicken stock (homemade or canned low-salt)
- 1/4 cup thinly sliced scallion (white and green parts), sliced on an angle
- Cilantro sprigs for garnish
- A few strips of julienned red bell pepper for garnish

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Soak the dried glass noodles and lily buds separately in warm water, and the cloud ear mushrooms in hot water, for 30 min. Drain well.
2. Heat the oil over high heat in a wok or large, heavy frying pan. Add the garlic and stir until it begins to color, then stir in the chicken and a dash of salt.
3. After a minute, push the chicken to the sides of the wok and crack the eggs into the center of the pan. Scramble the eggs briefly, then spoon the runny eggs onto the sides of the pan so they cook thinly and quickly.
4. Once the eggs have set, scrape them down and add the soaked noodles (cut them in half if very long), lily buds, cloud ears, celery, cabbage, carrots, dark or sweet soy sauce, pickled or roasted garlic, soy sauce, white pepper, and sugar, stirring well after each addition.
5. Stir in the chicken stock and mix well. The stock should be absorbed fairly quickly, within 2 to 3 min. Continue cooking and stirring until the noodles are clear and glossy.
6. Remove from heat and stir in the sliced scallion. Place on a serving plate and garnish with cilantro sprigs and red pepper.
