# Sauté of Monkfish with Ginger & Rum

I developed this dish after a visit to the West Indian island of Martinique, hence the rum. There they use cane syrup instead of honey. The meat glaze in the sauce is strictly French, but I think it adds real depth of flavor. You can omit it, but the character of the dish will be different-use a little more cream to make up the difference in volume of liquid. Monkfish is a very juicy fish, so I use a little flour and egg wash to help seal in the juices. My choice of wine for this dish would be a full-bodied, buttery California chardonnay or a rich French chardonnay like a Meursault. Serves four.

## Ingredients

- 1 1/2 lb. monkfish, cut into 1-in. slices
- 6 Tbs. olive oil
- 2 Tbs. grated fresh ginger
- 1 Tbs. crushed black peppercorns
- 1 Tbs. chopped fresh parsley
- Lemon juice
- Flour
- Egg wash (1 egg beaten with a few drops of olive oil and lemon juice, and salt and pepper)
- 3 Tbs. dark rum
- 6 Tbs. meat glaze
- 2 tsp. honey
- 1/2 cup heavy cream
- 1 Tbs. chopped fresh chives

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Gently press the monkfish slices to flatten them slightly and pat dry. Mix half the olive oil with the ginger, peppercorns, parsley, and 2 tsp. lemon juice in a shallow dish.
2. Arrange the monkfish in the dish, turning to coat with the marinade, and leave for 30 to 60 min. Remove the fish and wipe off all marinade.
3. Spread some flour on a piece of foil and put the egg wash in a shallow dish. Dip each piece of monkfish first in flour, shaking off the excess, then in egg wash, immediately before cooking (the coating gets gooey if left to sit).
4. Heat the remaining 3 Tbs. olive oil in a sauté pan and, when hot, add the monkfish. Sauté over medium-high heat until light brown, about 3 min. per side, then remove the fish from the pan.
5. Pour off the fat, return the fish to the pan, and add the rum. If flaming, stand back, dip a lighted match into the pan, and shake the pan until the flames subside; otherwise just simmer about 30 seconds. Remove the fish again and keep it warm.
6. Add the meat glaze and bring it to a boil, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Add the honey and cream and boil for a few minutes to reduce slightly. Season with salt, pepper, and more lemon juice to taste (the sauce should have a nice acidic edge).
7. If the fish needs reheating, put it back in the pan and heat for a few seconds in the sauce. Otherwise, arrange the fish on a warmed platter or individual plates and pour the sauce over it. Sprinkle with chopped chives.
