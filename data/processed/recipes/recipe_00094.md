# Jamaican Jerk Rub

You'll get a better texture if you chop the herbs and peppers by hand. A food processor or blender is a time-saver, but it tends to make an overly smooth puree. Be careful with the peppers-their oils can really burn your eyes and skin, so wash your hands when you're finished chopping, and don't add the seeds unless you like a lot of heat. Jerk rub is best after a few hours, when its flavors have had a chance to meld. The rub keeps for up to three months in the refrigerator. Makes 3 cups.

## Ingredients

- 30 scallions
- 5-in. piece fresh ginger, peeled
- 1/4 cup fresh garlic cloves, peeled
- 6 bay leaves
- 6 to 8 Scotch bonnet peppers, seeded, chopped fine by hand
- 6 to 8 habanero peppers, seeded, chopped fine by hand
- 2 tsp. freshly ground nutmeg, preferably Jamaican
- 2 tsp. freshly ground cinnamon (about 1 stick)
- 1 Tbs. freshly ground allspice, preferably Jamaican
- 2 Tbs. black peppercorns, ground fresh
- 2 Tbs. whole coriander seeds, ground fresh
- 1 Tbs. sea salt
- 1 Tbs. kosher salt
- 1 cup fresh thyme leaves, chopped fine by hand
- 1/2 cup oil (only if using a blender)
- 1 tsp. apricot jam (only if using habaneros instead of Scotch bonnets)
- 1 tsp. honey (only if using habaneros instead of Scotch bonnets)

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. If grinding your own spices, start with the nutmeg and cinnamon in a coffee mill or spice grinder and grind until coarse, then add the remaining spices and grind together until fine.
2. Chop the scallions, ginger, garlic, bay leaves, and peppers separately until moderately fine. (If using a food processor or blender instead of hand-chopping, pour in the oil first, then add these ingredients-but still chop the thyme and peppers by hand and add them at the end.)
3. Combine the scallions, ginger, garlic, bay leaves, and peppers and chop together until fine.
4. Put the mixture in a bowl and add the ground spices and salt.
5. Stir in the chopped thyme (and the apricot jam or honey, if using habaneros) and mix well.
