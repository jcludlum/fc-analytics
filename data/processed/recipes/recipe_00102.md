# Home-Smoked Fish

Smoking transforms the flavor and texture of fresh fish in a most felicitous way. Three techniques are suitable for home cooks: a covered kettle barbecue, a single-purpose stovetop smoker box, and a Chinese wok. All three use hot-smoking (in the 200°F range), during which the temperature of the fish rises to at least 140°F, at which stage the protein coagulates to the firm, opaque consistency that defines cooked fish.

## Ingredients

- Fish fillets, steaks, or small whole fish (higher-fat fish such as salmon, trout, char, or whitefish work best)
- Salt (kosher or pickling salt)
- Sugar
- Mixed pickling spices
- Wood chips or sawdust (hickory, alder, oak, maple, or fruitwoods such as apple, pear, or cherry), for barbecue or stovetop smoker-box smoking
- 1/4 cup rice, for wok smoking
- 1/4 cup tea leaves (black or oolong), for wok smoking
- 1/4 cup brown sugar, for wok smoking
- Soy sauce
- Ginger, sliced and crushed
- Scallion, cut into 1-inch lengths and crushed

*** = component recipe

## Directions

1. Choose a fish: higher-fat fish (salmon, trout, char, whitefish, tuna, mackerel, bluefish, mullet, herring, sablefish, or sturgeon) stays moist and balances the smoke best. Smaller fish can be smoked whole; larger fish should be filleted, butterflied, or cut into steaks, always leaving the skin on to help hold the meat together.
2. Cure the fish with salt, or a blend of salt and sugar: for a dry cure, use about 2 tsp. total salt and sugar per pound of boneless fish, sprinkled directly onto the fish with a heavier layer on the thicker parts. For larger pieces, brine instead, using about 2 Tbs. total salt and sugar per cup of cold water in a sealed bag or nonreactive container, refrigerated.
3. After curing, soak the fish 15 minutes in water (or at least rinse it) to remove excess surface salt. If time permits, air-dry the fish 15 minutes or more in a cool, breezy place to form a thin skin (pellicle) before smoking.
4. Barbecue smoking: fillet the fish, leaving the skin on, and remove the pin bones. Dry-salt with three parts salt to one part sugar, cover, and refrigerate 8 hours or overnight. An hour before smoking, drain the accumulated juices, cover with cold water, let stand 15 minutes, drain, and repeat, then pat dry (air-dry on a rack 15 minutes for a smoother surface). Start a small charcoal fire (about a dozen briquettes) at one edge of a covered kettle barbecue and let it burn down to gray ash. Soak 1/2 cup wood chips in water, set the top and bottom vents about 90 percent closed, drain the chips, and add them to the coals. Lay the fish on the grill opposite the fire, thickest part nearest the heat. Cover and cook until the fish is opaque, 30 to 40 minutes, adjusting the vents to maintain 180° to 200°F and adding more dry chips after 15 minutes. Let cool, wrap, and chill before serving.
5. Stovetop smoker box: brine up to 2 lb. of fish in a straight salt brine for 20 minutes, drain, rinse with a couple of changes of fresh water, then brine a second time with half salt and half brown sugar plus a tablespoon or so of mixed pickling spices. Let stand 20 minutes, drain, and dry on a rack at least 15 minutes. Spread about 2 Tbs. of wood dust in the bottom of the smoker pan, set the drip pan and rack on top, lay the fish on the rack, cover, and cook over medium heat until the fish registers 140°F, 12 to 18 minutes depending on thickness.
6. Wok smoking: place the fish in a shallow bowl. Sprinkle with a tablespoon or two of soy sauce and a large pinch of sugar, and add the crushed ginger and scallion. Marinate 10 to 20 minutes, turning occasionally. Line the inside of a 14-inch or larger wok and its lid with heavy-duty foil, letting the excess drape over the rim. Combine the rice, tea leaves, and brown sugar and spread in a 6-inch circle in the center of the wok. Set a wire rack in place over the mixture and turn the heat to high. When the sugar begins to smolder, drain the fish, wipe off the excess marinade, and set it on the rack. Put the lid on and crimp the foil layers together to hold in the smoke. Reduce the heat to medium-high and cook 10 minutes for thin fillets or butterflied fish, 12 minutes for thick steaks. Turn off the heat, carefully unfold the foil, and test the fish for doneness with a skewer in the thickest part; it should penetrate evenly and easily. Serve hot.
